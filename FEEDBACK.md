<summary>Full AAPOR award citation</summary>

The AAPOR Award is the Association's lifetime achievement award. It is given for an outstanding contribution to the field of public opinion research, including: advances in theory, empirical research and methods; improvements in ethical standards; and promotion of understanding among the public, media and/or policy makers.

Timothy P. Johnson's leadership, scholarship, mentorship, and advocacy have enriched public opinion and survey research for over 35 years. His substantive and methodological contributions, his promotion and enhancement of research standards, and his advocacy for the importance of survey research, are each remarkable in their own right. Collectively, they have profoundly enhanced our entire field.

An enormously productive scholar, Tim's impactful publications, including seven edited volumes and hundreds of refereed articles and book chapters, have advanced the science of survey measurement, improved representation of hard-to-count populations, and produced innovations in cross-cultural methodology. Moreover, his ground-breaking applications of survey data to the study of health behavior, health care, substance abuse, and mental health have reflected his commitment to using social science to promote the common good, particularly the health and well-being of underserved populations.

Tim's generosity to both students and colleagues is legendary. Through decades of teaching and serving on over 80 dissertation committees at the University of Illinois at Chicago, he has developed the methodological finesse of a whole generation of researchers. He has also answered countless calls to serve on advisory, review, organizing and service committees across a wide range of academic and professional institutions.

Tim's executive leadership service across our profession is almost unparalleled, including the presidencies of AAPOR, WAPOR, MAPOR, and AASRO. Across these organizations, he tirelessly protected our professional interests through advocacy for the importance of polls and surveys for informed decision-making and combatting misinformation, as well as promoting establishment of and adherence to strong research standards. His leadership in AAPOR's Transparency Initiative, including development of requirements, training, and outreach plans, and overall implementation of its vision, were essential to its success.

For all his extraordinary achievements and contributions, the American Association for Public Opinion Research is pleased to present the 2023 Award for Exceptionally Distinguished Achievement to Dr. Timothy P. Johnson.

<summary>Full retirement note (source text)</summary>

I write to inform you of the retirement of Tim Johnson, long-time director of the Survey Research Laboratory (SRL) and professor of Public Administration.

Tim was recruited to UIC in 1989 and we have been grateful recipients of his work and collegiality ever since. Tim set the highest standards for research and ethics at UIC and his influence extends well beyond the university. He is truly an internationally-recognized scholar, a person who is a role model to other scholars, and a scholar/administrator who performs at the highest level. Tim has the highest regard from his staff at SRL and all colleagues around the university. I dare say that Tim is one of the kindest, most thoughtful, and generous scholars in the academy.

Tim holds the titles of research professor in UIC's Epidemiology & Biostatistics Department in the School of Public Health; deputy director for evaluation and tracking in UIC's Center for Clinical Translational Science, and vice chair on UIC's Institutional Review Board (IRB) #2 (Social and Behavioral) since 1998. As a member of the IRB, Tim was absolutely instrumental in addressing UIC's protection of research subjects since that time.

After being recruited in 1989 as a project coordinator in SRL, Tim advanced to the position of director of the Lab's operations in 1996 that for many years spanned the three campuses of the University of Illinois System. At the same time, Tim achieved a world-renowned reputation for excellence in survey research methodology and in particular in pioneering multi-cultural survey research. Throughout his career, Tim served as principal investigator or co-investigator on grants from funders including NIH, NSF, CDC, the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation among many others, which have generated almost $30 million in research dollars to UIC. And he worked tirelessly with faculty and centers across the campus in a collaborative and co-investigative role, most notably with the health sciences, as well as offering advice and consultation to anyone who asked.

Tim has published extensively in the field of survey research and has presented in a multitude of venues and to such communities as academic, government, and lay professionals around the globe. During his 30 years at UIC, he has presented hundreds of papers, published 200 articles in refereed journals and has authored or edited seven books, one of which won the 2013 book award from the Association of Academic Survey Research Organizations. Although this represents a remarkable productivity rate for scholars in survey research, it is even more remarkable that his productivity as a scholar is extraordinary in light of the fact that he has been a center director for much of his tenure at UIC.

Tim's main areas of expertise include survey methodology and health behaviors in disadvantaged populations. Within the field of survey methodology, his work focused primarily on sources of measurement and nonresponse error. His measurement error work is concerned with cultural variability in the cognitive processing of survey questions, an area in which much of his work has been invested for nearly two decades, marking him as one of the pioneers in this area. In particular, he advanced the profession's understanding of survey respondent's race, ethnic, or national identity in their responses to survey questions. He also conducted numerous investigations designed to validate self-reported health information.

His work on nonresponse error has been directed at developing new methods for identifying the effects of nonresponse mechanisms on the quality of survey estimates, as well as the development and testing of new approaches to adjusting for nonresponse bias.

Tim has also invested considerable effort in examining the health behaviors of disadvantaged populations. He has worked with homeless persons, immigrants, cultural minorities, criminal justice populations and mental health populations.

Tim's commitment to enhancing survey research in the region and the world as well as the international significance of his research contribution to the academy is noted in his many recognitions. He was named a fellow by the American Statistical Association for his work exploring cross-cultural issues in survey research in 2015. One year prior, he was named fellow by the Midwest Association of Public Opinion Research. In 2018, he received the Association of Academic Survey Research Organization's John M. Kennedy Achievement Award for his service and leadership to academic survey research. He also served as president of the American Association for Public Opinion Research, a professional organization of public opinion and survey research professionals within the U.S. with members from academia, government, nonprofits, and industry.

We are better scholars, better researchers, and kinder colleagues because of the example set by Tim. I will miss his regular presence in CUPPA. I wish him much happiness and fun in his future endeavors as a brilliant scholar, new grandfather, and long-time Eagle Scout.
